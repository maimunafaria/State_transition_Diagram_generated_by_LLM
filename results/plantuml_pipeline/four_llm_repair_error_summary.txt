=============================

-----

- Qwen 2.5 7B Instruct: Original RAG

Cases per model: 27
Total model-case combinations: 108

Repair methods:

All comparisons in this report use the same frozen initial diagrams for both
repair methods. A violation is counted once per case, even if several states
or transitions in that case exhibit the same violation.

Plain-language interpretation
-----------------------------

Across all four models, Syntax-Grounded Repair achieved the higher overall
strict-validity result: 63 of 108 diagrams (58.33%), compared with 58 of 108
(53.70%) for Baseline Repair.

Syntax-Grounded Repair also solved more total violation-case occurrences:
103 of 161 (63.98%), compared with 86 of 161 (53.42%) for Baseline Repair.

Syntax-Grounded Repair was particularly effective for:

- Orphan states: 14 of 14 solved.
- Invalid [*] to [*] transitions: 2 of 2 solved.
- Choice nodes without guarded transitions: 5 of 6 solved.
- Missing initial transitions: 11 of 14 solved.
- Unreachable states: 11 of 19 solved.
- Multiple initial transitions: 10 of 25 solved, compared with 5 for baseline.

Baseline Repair was more effective for:

- Missing final transitions: 40 of 49 solved.
- Duplicate transitions: 7 of 10 solved.
- Choice nodes without outgoing transitions: 5 of 10 solved.

PlantUML syntax errors were difficult for both methods. Baseline solved only
2 of 12 initial syntax-error cases, while Syntax-Grounded Repair solved 3 of
12. Syntax-Grounded Repair also introduced six new syntax-error occurrences,
compared with two introduced by Baseline Repair.

Model dependence
----------------

Syntax-Grounded Repair improved Qwen, Mistral, and Llama, but reduced
DeepSeek's strict validity from 88.89% to 81.48%. Therefore, the experiment
does not support claiming that Syntax-Grounded Repair is universally better
for every model.

Final conclusion
----------------

Syntax-Grounded Repair is the stronger method overall, but its effectiveness
depends on the violation type and model.

The best practical strategy suggested by these results is a hybrid repair
policy:

- Prefer Syntax-Grounded Repair for orphan states, missing initial states,
  multiple initial states, unreachable states, guarded-choice problems, and
  invalid [*] to [*] transitions.
- Prefer Baseline Repair for missing final transitions, duplicate
  transitions, and choice nodes without outgoing transitions.
- Add a dedicated deterministic or compiler-guided stage for PlantUML syntax
  errors, because both LLM-based repair methods perform poorly on syntax and
  can introduce new syntax errors.
